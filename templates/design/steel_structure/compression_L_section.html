Compression member (single angle) design

Same trial-and-error logic as the W-shape tool, applied to a single equal-leg angle. The governing radius of gyration for an unrestrained single angle is rz — the minimum radius, about the geometric (z-z) axis — not rx or ry. Section 02 lets you assume a trial KL/r and back out a target Ag and rz. Section 03 checks a real angle's actual capacity. Section 04 logs your trials so you can pick the lightest one that passes.
Pn = Fcr · Ag  |  Fcr = 0.658^(Fy/Fe)·Fy for KL/r ≤ 4.71√(E/Fy), else Fcr = 0.877Fe  |  Fe = π²E/(KL/r)²  |  r = rz (min. radius of gyration)
Caveat: AISC Section E5 gives single angles a special modified slenderness ratio (KL/r)mod that accounts for the eccentricity of a connection through one leg — it can raise or lower the effective KL/r depending on end-restraint case (a), (b), or (c). This calculator applies the general E3 flexural-buckling method with the actual rz, load applied through the centroid, mirroring the W-shape tool's simplification. Treat results here as a first pass; apply E5 for a final single-angle design with eccentric end connections.

02 · Trial iteration — assume KL/r, find required Ag

Since Fcr depends only on KL/r (not on the section itself), assume a slenderness ratio, compute Fcr, then back out the required gross area. Look for an angle with rz ≥ the required r below and Ag ≥ the required area — then check it for real in section 03. Adjust the assumed KL/r and repeat until it stops improving.
