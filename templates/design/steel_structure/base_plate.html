Base Plates & HSS Bolted End-Plate Connections

Pick a design mode below. Mode 1 is the AISC ASD concentric-axial-load column base plate procedure. Mode 2 is the round HSS bolted circular end-plate connection under bending moment (Steel Tube Institute / Packer, 2023 — based on Wheeler et al. 1998).

Sheet 01Inputs

Partial implementation. The source article (STI / Packer, 2023) renders Eqs. (1) and (2) — the actual bolt-failure and plate-plastification moment capacities — as embedded graphics. The parent design model (Wheeler et al. 1998, for two-row rectangular-HSS end plates) is available in the open literature, but its equations assume a two-bolt-row geometry rotating about a far row, whereas the round-HSS conversion here rotates about a single extreme-compression point on the tube — the two are not related by a simple substitution. Rather than reconstruct a load-bearing formula I can't verify, those two equations are still not computed below. Everything else — geometry, effective bolts, the plate-thickness limit of applicability (Eq. 3), and the AISC J3 bolt spacing/edge-distance checks — is computed and verified against the article's worked example (543 / 659 kip-in., HSS 6.625×0.375, 6 bolts, 7/8" A325).
HSS Member
Bolts
F_yb here follows ASTM F3125 Table 5 (92 ksi for A325). Where not directly known, F_yb ≈ 0.77·F_ub is a reasonable estimate (per ASTM F3125 minimum yield/tensile ratio).
End Plate
a = (plate OD − bolt PCD)/2, b = (bolt PCD − D)/2. Defaults reproduce the article's example: a = b = 1.50 in. for a 12.625" OD plate, 9.625" bolt circle, on a 6.625" OD tube.
Demand (Applied Load)
M_u = 1.2·M_DL + 1.6·M_LL (ASCE 7 basic LRFD combination), kip-in. Compared below against the HSS member's design flexural strength as a reference check — see the caveat in Sheet 02/03 about what this check does and does not cover. Edit M_DL/M_LL above; M_u updates automatically. If you already have a governing factored M_u from another load combination, you can type directly into the M_u field to override.
