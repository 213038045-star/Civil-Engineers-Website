Pile Group & Pile Cap Design

SPT-based pile capacity (skin friction α/β-method + end bearing, per AASHTO 2007) and pile cap design for tower foundations with 2, 3, 4, 5, or 6 piles per group. Units: kN, m, mm, MPa. Structural checks cover punching (two-way) shear, one-way shear, and flexural design; more specialized checks (column-face shear-friction, hook length, pedestal design) are out of scope for this tool — see footnote.

Preliminary design tool for educational / early‑stage use. Pile capacity: cumulative layer‑by‑layer skin friction (α‑method for clay: Su=6N, α per the standard reduction beyond Su/101>1.5; β‑method for sand: β=1.5−0.245√z, scaled by N/15 when N≤15) plus end bearing (9·Su for clay; depth/diameter‑dependent Meyerhof‑type correlation for sand), per AASHTO 2007 — matching the assumptions noted in the source workbook. Required pile length is solved by linear interpolation of the soil‑capacity‑vs‑depth curve against total group demand (compression = tower C + cap/pedestal self‑weight; uplift = tower U − self‑weight), then rounded up to the next 0.5 m with a 1 m safety margin, exactly as in the source. Cap geometry for each pile count follows the source workbook's formulas, with pile‑to‑pile and pile‑to‑edge distances entered as multiples of pile diameter. Punching and one‑way shear use an equivalent smeared pile‑reaction pressure (total factored pile load ÷ cap plan area) rather than checking each individual pile position — a standard simplification for compact pile groups (this file's default 2.5D spacing) but a coarser approximation for widely‑spaced piles, where individual‑pile punching per ACI 318 §13.2.7 should be checked separately. Flexural design uses the source's exact cantilever‑moment formula (Mu = LF·(P·spacing/4 + H·pedestal height)) and its ACI 10.5.3‑style minimum‑reinforcement cascade (including the 4/3·As provision). Not covered by this tool: column‑face shear‑friction checks (CRSI guideline), hook length past the edge pile, and pedestal design (slenderness, main bars, ties) — verify these separately. Always confirm against a project‑specific geotechnical report and have the final design checked/sealed by a licensed structural engineer.
