Underground Water Reservoir — Base Slab Design

Sizes a rectangular underground service reservoir from demand, then designs the base slab as a two‑way restrained slab for the worst‑case condition (tank empty, groundwater table at ground level). Follows the same layout logic as a typical hand calculation: capacity → tank dimensions → uplift vs. self‑weight → net design pressure → panel moments → depth/steel design → minimum steel.

Preliminary design tool for educational / early‑stage use. Worst-case uplift condition assumed: tank empty, groundwater table at ground level. Base slab treated as a two‑way restrained rectangular panel spanning between the tank's interior wall faces, using user‑supplied moment coefficients (BNBC/ACI 350‑style tables) — always confirm the coefficients match your actual edge‑restraint case and span ratio m = A/B. Depth requirement is solved directly from the assumed ρ = ρ_max so the section stays under‑reinforced (ductile); flexural steel area is then solved separately by a/A_s trial‑and‑error iteration for the actual moment demand in each direction. Minimum steel uses one explicit ρ_min on the gross section (temperature & shrinkage) rather than mixing coefficients. Always verify against your project's geotechnical report and load combinations, and have the final design checked/sealed by a licensed structural engineer before construction.
