Reference basis & assumptions

  • Design equation: log10(W18) = ZR·S0 + 9.36·log10(SN+1) − 0.2 + [log10(ΔPSI/2.7)] / [0.4 + 1094/(SN+1)^5.19] + 2.32·log10(MR) − 8.07 — the 1993 AASHTO Guide for Design of Pavement Structures flexible pavement equation. It's implicit in SN, so this tool solves it by bisection.
  • The 2.7 in the ΔPSI term is a fixed constant (4.2 − 1.5, from the original AASHO Road Test), not your actual p0 − pt — that difference is the numerator, ΔPSI, which you do control.
  • Layered design method: SN1 (protects the base) is solved using the base course's own resilient modulus in place of "subgrade" MR; SN2 (protects the subbase) uses the subbase's MR; SN3 (protects the subgrade) uses the actual subgrade MR. Required thicknesses follow as D1 ≥ SN1/a1, then D2 from (SN2 − a1D1)/(a2m2), then D3 from (SN3 − a1D1 − a2m2D2)/(a3m3).
  • Typical layer coefficients (a1 ≈ 0.44/in for dense asphalt concrete, a2 ≈ 0.10–0.14/in for granular base, a3 ≈ 0.08–0.11/in for granular subbase) and drainage coefficients (m ≈ 0.4–1.4 depending on drainage quality and exposure) are starting points — use project-specific lab/test values where available.
  • Minimum thickness guidance follows the traffic-level bands commonly published from AASHTO 1993 Guide Table 3.1; treat it as a floor, not a substitute for the SN checks above.
This tool implements the classic 1993 AASHTO empirical method for preliminary/educational sizing. It does not cover mechanistic-empirical (MEPDG) analysis, seasonal MR adjustment, frost/swell effects, or overlay design. Have the final pavement design verified by a licensed engineer familiar with local subgrade and climate conditions before construction.
