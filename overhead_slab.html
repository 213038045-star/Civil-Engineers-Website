Rooftop (Overhead) Water Tank — Base Slab Design

Base slab of an elevated rooftop RCC tank, designed as a two‑way panel spanning between 4 edge beams. Unlike an underground reservoir, there is no soil bearing check and no buoyancy/uplift condition — the slab simply carries the water column plus its own self‑weight downward onto the supporting beams.

Assumption: base slab support = 4 edge beams (you didn't specify a preference, so this is the most common textbook case for a small RCC rooftop tank). If your tank actually rests flat on the roof slab, or sits on a column staging, the span/support logic here won't apply directly — let me know and I'll rebuild it for that case.

Preliminary design tool for educational / early‑stage use. Base slab treated as a two‑way panel spanning L × B between edge beams, carrying factored self‑weight + water load only (no soil bearing, no uplift/buoyancy check — those are specific to buried reservoirs). Depth requirement solved from ρ = ρ_max (keeps section ductile); flexural steel solved separately by a/A_s trial‑and‑error for actual moment demand. Minimum steel uses one explicit ρ_min on the gross section. This sheet does not size the supporting beams, columns, or staging — that is a separate design. Always verify against your project's loading code and have the final design checked/sealed by a licensed structural engineer before construction.
