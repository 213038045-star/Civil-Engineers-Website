Reference basis & assumptions

  • Design equation: log10(W18) = ZR·S0 + 7.35·log10(D+1) − 0.06 + [log10(ΔPSI/3.0)] / [1 + 1.624×10⁷/(D+1)^8.46] + (4.22 − 0.32·pt)·log10{ [Sc′·Cd·(D^0.75 − 1.132)] / [215.63·J·(D^0.75 − 18.42/(Ec/k)^0.25)] } — the 1993 AASHTO Guide for Design of Pavement Structures rigid pavement equation. It's implicit in slab thickness D, so this tool solves it by bisection.
  • The 3.0 in the ΔPSI term is a fixed constant (4.5 − 1.5, from the original AASHO Road Test scale for PCC), not your actual p0 − pt — that difference is the numerator, ΔPSI, which you do control.
  • k is the effective, composite modulus of subgrade reaction — it already reflects the subgrade plus any subbase and any loss of support (LS) adjustment. The AASHTO Guide derives it from a nomograph combining subgrade MR, subbase modulus/thickness, and LS; entering it directly is the standard approach for this method. The MR/19.4 figure shown alongside it is only a rough order-of-magnitude cross-check for a bare subgrade with no subbase, not a substitute for the nomograph or a nondestructive-testing-based estimate.
  • Unlike the flexible method, rigid design does not use a layered structural-number build-up — the PCC slab alone carries the structural check. Base/subbase layers under a slab are mainly there to control pumping, erosion, and construction support, and are shown in the drawing for reference but are not part of the AASHTO SN-style check.
  • Typical inputs: Sc′ ≈ 550–750 psi (28-day third-point flexural strength), Ec ≈ 3–5×10⁶ psi, J ≈ 2.6–3.2 for doweled joints with a tied/AC shoulder (higher, up to ~4.0, for undoweled joints and no shoulder), Cd ≈ 0.70–1.25 depending on drainage quality and time of near-saturation exposure.
  • AASHTO 1993 does not publish a formal minimum-PCC-thickness table by traffic level the way it does for flexible pavements (its Table 3.1). The bands below reflect commonly cited practical minimums from agency practice, not a formal Guide table — treat them as a sanity floor, not a substitute for the slab check above.
This tool implements the classic 1993 AASHTO empirical rigid pavement method for preliminary/educational sizing. It does not cover joint spacing/faulting design, dowel bar sizing, reinforcement (for JRCP/CRCP), CRCP-specific analysis, or mechanistic-empirical (MEPDG) analysis. Have the final pavement design verified by a licensed engineer familiar with local subgrade and climate conditions before construction.
