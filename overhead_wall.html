Rooftop (Overhead) Water Tank — Sidewall Design

Designs the elevated tank sidewall in two zones: a free vertical cantilever over the top height h (unsupported at top, e.g. before the roof slab engages), and a two‑way restrained panel over the remaining height H−h spanning horizontally between the long and short walls. Unlike a buried reservoir, there is no external soil pressure — loading is internal hydrostatic water pressure only.

Note: adapted from the underground-reservoir sidewall sheet with the soil-pressure term removed — the tank wall here only ever sees water pushing out from the inside, never earth pushing in. If your rooftop tank staging or exposure means wind or seismic sloshing forces also load the walls, those aren't covered here and should be checked separately.

Sidewall split into a free-cantilever top zone (height h) and a restrained two-way panel zone (height H−h) per common tank-wall design practice; the boundary between them and the exact panel moment coefficients depend on your governing code's edge-restraint assumptions — confirm both before finalizing. Loading is internal hydrostatic water pressure only (no soil term), appropriate for an above-ground/rooftop tank — if wind or seismic sloshing loads govern for your staging height and exposure, check those separately. As_min (temperature & shrinkage) is applied consistently to all three sections (cantilever, long wall, short wall) using one explicit ρ_min on the gross wall thickness. φ = 0.9 assumed throughout for flexure. This sheet does not size the supporting beams, columns, or staging. Always verify against your governing code and have the final design checked/sealed by a licensed structural engineer before construction.
